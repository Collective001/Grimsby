Entry XIII

The Offsite Journal of Elias Grimsby

The Second Sunday Since Disembarkation, Pending Botanical Confirmation

My investigation in Maine has yielded several promising discoveries.

However...

Reports have emerged from New York City of a peculiar creature causing disturbances in several neighborhoods.

Witnesses were unable to recall what the creature looked like.

They were, however, able to recall its affection for confections with remarkable clarity.

I suspect this is Subject H-31.

I must depart immediately.

I was initially troubled to learn that H-31 had taken up residence in a replacement of the original York City.

I had not been informed that the original required replacing.

I approached a coach driver and insisted that he take me to the latest version of York City.

He stared at me quizzically.

I stared back.

He blinked.

I changed my approach and inquired whether there was a New York City.

He responded by telling me about fruit.

I stared at him.

He stared back.

I asked whether the fruit was important.

He blinked.

Then I remembered that America is a country of remarkable roadside wonders.

Apparently, New York City is home to the World's Largest Apple.

This is an extraordinary botanical achievement.

I agreed to make the journey.

I am headed to Malus grandis.

Or perhaps Malus noveboracensis?

I shall discover this upon my arrival.

I do hope the apple is still attached to its tree.

I should very much like to see the tree.

We departed shortly thereafter.

The American countryside is quite pleasant.

There are trees.

Fields.

Occasional buildings.

I observed several cows.

They appeared to be ordinary cows.

I found this reassuring.

Our journey continued without incident until the coach stopped briefly along the road.

A rather dour gentleman stood nearby.

I inquired whether he was also traveling to see the giant apple.

He stared at me.

I stared back.

He seemed a rather grumpy fellow.

"I want to be in the room where it happens," he replied.

I found this an oddly specific ambition.

I wished him success in his endeavors.

The coach continued onward.

I have spent much of the journey considering the implications of an apple of such extraordinary size.

The tree required to produce such a specimen must be enormous.

Its root system alone would warrant considerable study.

I wonder whether the fruit is edible.

And whether the locals have attempted to make a pie.

Such an undertaking would require an exceptionally large oven.

Perhaps this explains the construction of the new city.

I shall investigate.

We have finally arrived.

The buildings are quite tall.

The streets are crowded.

There is a remarkable amount of noise.

I have yet to locate the apple.

Or its tree.

However...

I suspect an object of such extraordinary dimensions should not be difficult to find.

I shall begin my investigation in the morning.

Signed, Elias Grimsby The Grimsby Institute
